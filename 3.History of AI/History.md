### **Notes: The History of Artificial Intelligence**

**1. The Birth of AI (1950s)**

- **Alan Turing (1950):** Shifted the debate on machine intelligence from philosophy to experimental science by proposing the *Turing Test* (0:31-1:33).
- **Dartmouth Conference (1956):** The official establishment of *Artificial Intelligence* as a formal academic field, where experts gathered to explore simulating human intelligence via computers (1:34-2:09).

**2. The AI Winter (1960s – 1970s)**

- A period of stagnant progress characterized by a lack of sufficient computing power and limited data availability, which caused a decline in research funding and interest (2:43-2:59).

**3. The Resurgence (1997 – 2011)**

- **IBM Deep Blue (1997):** A major psychological and technical milestone where an AI defeated world chess champion *Gary Kasparov* (3:00-3:06).
- **Rise of Data & Compute:** The explosion of the internet and processing power in the late 90s provided the fuel needed for modern AI (3:08-3:29).
- **Neural Network Revival (2006):** *Geoffrey Hinton* published research that brought *Deep Learning* back into the spotlight (3:33-3:43).
- **IBM Watson (2011):** Won the *Jeopardy!* quiz show, demonstrating significant leaps in *Natural Language Processing* (4:00-4:13).

**4. The Generative Era (2012 – Present)**

- **Breakthroughs in Vision (2012):** Researchers at *Google* and *Stanford* successfully trained deep neural networks to recognize complex objects like cats (4:16-4:35).
- **Transformers (2017):** The *Google Brain* team introduced *Transformers*, which utilize 'self-attention' to process sequences of data efficiently (4:37-4:56).
- **LLMs and ChatGPT (2018–2022):** The development of the *GPT* series led to powerful *Large Language Models (LLMs)*, culminating in the release of user-friendly interfaces like *ChatGPT* in late 2022 (4:57-5:27).
